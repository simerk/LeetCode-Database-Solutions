## 🚀 1867. Orders With Maximum Quantity Above Average 🔒

### 📖 Problem

You are running an e-commerce site that is looking for **imbalanced orders**. An **imbalanced order** is one whose **maximum** quantity is **strictly greater** than the **average** quantity of **every order (including itself)**.

The **average** quantity of an order is calculated as `(total quantity of all products in the order) / (number of different products in the order)`. The **maximum** quantity of an order is the highest `quantity` of any single product in the order.

Write a solution to find the `order_id` of all **imbalanced orders**.

Return the result table in **any order**.

---

### 📝 Example

```text
Input: 
OrdersDetails table:
+----------+------------+----------+
| order_id | product_id | quantity |
+----------+------------+----------+
| 1        | 1          | 12       |
| 1        | 2          | 10       |
| 1        | 3          | 15       |
| 2        | 1          | 8        |
| 2        | 4          | 4        |
| 2        | 5          | 6        |
| 3        | 3          | 5        |
| 3        | 4          | 18       |
| 4        | 5          | 2        |
| 4        | 6          | 8        |
| 5        | 7          | 9        |
| 5        | 8          | 9        |
| 3        | 9          | 20       |
| 2        | 9          | 4        |
+----------+------------+----------+
Output: 
+----------+
| order_id |
+----------+
| 1        |
| 3        |
+----------+
Explanation: 
The average quantity of each order is:
- order_id=1: (12+10+15)/3 = 12.3333333
- order_id=2: (8+4+6+4)/4 = 5.5
- order_id=3: (5+18+20)/3 = 14.333333
- order_id=4: (2+8)/2 = 5
- order_id=5: (9+9)/2 = 9

The maximum quantity of each order is:
- order_id=1: max(12, 10, 15) = 15
- order_id=2: max(8, 4, 6, 4) = 8
- order_id=3: max(5, 18, 20) = 20
- order_id=4: max(2, 8) = 8
- order_id=5: max(9, 9) = 9

Orders 1 and 3 are imbalanced because they have a maximum quantity that exceeds the average quantity of every order.
```
---

### 💡 Idea

For each order, calculate its **average quantity** and **maximum quantity**.

An order is imbalanced when its maximum quantity is strictly greater than its average quantity.

---

### 💻 Solution

```sql
WITH order_stats AS (
    SELECT order_id,
    AVG(quantity) AS avg_quantity,
    MAX(quantity) AS max_quantity
    FROM OrdersDetails
    GROUP BY order_id
)
SELECT order_id
FROM order_stats
WHERE max_quantity > avg_quantity;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

1. Group all products belonging to the same order.
2. `AVG(quantity)` calculates the average quantity of products in the order.
3. `MAX(quantity)` finds the largest quantity of a single product.
4. Keep orders where the maximum quantity is strictly greater than the average.
5. Each order appears only once in the result.

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(n)` *(scans all order details and aggregates them by order)*

**Space Complexity:** `O(k)` *(stores statistics for `k` distinct orders)*
