## 🚀 3421. Find Students Who Improved

### 📖 Problem

Write a solution to find the **students who have shown improvement**. A student is considered to have shown improvement if they meet both of these conditions:

- Have taken exams in the **same subject** on at least two different dates
- Their **latest score** in that subject is **higher** than their first score
  
Return the result table ordered by `student_id`, `subject` in **ascending order**.

---

### 📝 Example
```text
Input:
Scores table:
+------------+----------+-------+------------+
| student_id | subject  | score | exam_date  |
+------------+----------+-------+------------+
| 101        | Math     | 70    | 2023-01-15 |
| 101        | Math     | 85    | 2023-02-15 |
| 101        | Physics  | 65    | 2023-01-15 |
| 101        | Physics  | 60    | 2023-02-15 |
| 102        | Math     | 80    | 2023-01-15 |
| 102        | Math     | 85    | 2023-02-15 |
| 103        | Math     | 90    | 2023-01-15 |
| 104        | Physics  | 75    | 2023-01-15 |
| 104        | Physics  | 85    | 2023-02-15 |
+------------+----------+-------+------------+
Output:
+------------+----------+-------------+--------------+
| student_id | subject  | first_score | latest_score |
+------------+----------+-------------+--------------+
| 101        | Math     | 70          | 85           |
| 102        | Math     | 80          | 85           |
| 104        | Physics  | 75          | 85           |
+------------+----------+-------------+--------------+
Explanation:
• Student 101 in Math: Improved from 70 to 85
• Student 101 in Physics: No improvement (dropped from 65 to 60)
• Student 102 in Math: Improved from 80 to 85
• Student 103 in Math: Only one exam, not eligible
• Student 104 in Physics: Improved from 75 to 85
Result table is ordered by student_id, subject.
```
---

### 💡 Idea

The goal is to find students whose **latest exam score** is higher than their **first exam score** for each subject.

We use the `ROW_NUMBER()` window function twice:

- Once to identify the **first exam**.
- Once to identify the **latest exam**.

Finally, we join both results and keep only the students whose latest score is greater than their first score.

---

## 💻 Solution

```sql
WITH first_exam AS (
    SELECT student_id, subject, score,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY student_id, subject ORDER BY exam_date) AS rn
    FROM Scores
),

latest_exam AS (
    SELECT student_id, subject, score,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY student_id, subject ORDER BY exam_date DESC) AS rn
    FROM Scores
),

SELECT f.student_id, f.subject,
f.score AS first_score, l.score AS latest_score
FROM first_exam f
JOIN latest_exam l
ON f.student_id = l.student_id AND f.subject = l.subject
WHERE f.rn = 1 AND l.rn = 1 AND l.score > f.score
ORDER BY f.student_id, f.subject;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

1. Use `ROW_NUMBER()` to identify the **first exam** for each student and subject.
2. Use another `ROW_NUMBER()` to identify the **latest exam**.
3. Join both CTEs on `student_id` and `subject`.
4. Keep only the rows where the latest score is greater than the first score.

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(n log n)`

**Space Complexity:** `O(n)`
