## 🚀 1205. Monthly Transactions II 🔒

### 📖 Problem

Write a solution to find for each month and country: the number of approved transactions and their total amount, the number of chargebacks, and their total amount.

**Note:** In your solution, given the month and country, ignore rows with all zeros.

Return the result table in **any order**.

---

### 📝 Example
```text
Input: 
Transactions table:                                     Chargebacks table:
+-----+---------+----------+--------+------------+      +----------+------------+
| id  | country | state    | amount | trans_date |      | trans_id | trans_date |
+-----+---------+----------+--------+------------+      +----------+------------+
| 101 | US      | approved | 1000   | 2019-05-18 |      | 102      | 2019-05-29 |
| 102 | US      | declined | 2000   | 2019-05-19 |      | 101      | 2019-06-30 |
| 103 | US      | approved | 3000   | 2019-06-10 |      | 105      | 2019-09-18 |
| 104 | US      | declined | 4000   | 2019-06-13 |      +----------+------------+
| 105 | US      | approved | 5000   | 2019-06-15 |
+-----+---------+----------+--------+------------+
Output: 
+---------+---------+----------------+-----------------+------------------+-------------------+
| month   | country | approved_count | approved_amount | chargeback_count | chargeback_amount |
+---------+---------+----------------+-----------------+------------------+-------------------+
| 2019-05 | US      | 1              | 1000            | 1                | 2000              |
| 2019-06 | US      | 2              | 8000            | 1                | 1000              |
| 2019-09 | US      | 0              | 0               | 1                | 5000              |
+---------+---------+----------------+-----------------+------------------+-------------------+
```
---

### 💡 Idea

We need to combine **approved transactions** with their corresponding **chargebacks**, grouped by month and country.

There are two important cases:

* Approved transactions contribute to `approved_count` and `approved_amount`.
* Chargebacks contribute to `chargeback_count` and `chargeback_amount`, using the **original transaction's country and amount**, but the **chargeback date's month**.

We can use `UNION ALL` to create both types of records and then aggregate them.

---

### 💻 Solution

```sql
SELECT month, country,
SUM(approved_count) AS approved_count,
SUM(approved_amount) AS approved_amount,
SUM(chargeback_count) AS chargeback_count,
SUM(chargeback_amount) AS chargeback_amount
FROM (
    -- Approved transactions
    SELECT
        DATE_FORMAT(trans_date, '%Y-%m') AS month,
        country,
        1 AS approved_count,
        amount AS approved_amount,
        0 AS chargeback_count,
        0 AS chargeback_amount
    FROM Transactions
    WHERE state = 'approved'

    UNION ALL

    -- Chargebacks
    SELECT
        DATE_FORMAT(c.trans_date, '%Y-%m') AS month,
        t.country,
        0 AS approved_count,
        0 AS approved_amount,
        1 AS chargeback_count,
        t.amount AS chargeback_amount
    FROM Chargebacks c
    JOIN Transactions t ON c.trans_id = t.id
) AS combined
GROUP BY month, country
HAVING approved_count + approved_amount + chargeback_count + chargeback_amount > 0;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

1. Select all approved transactions and assign their counts and amounts.
2. Join `Chargebacks` with `Transactions` to get the original country's and transaction amount.
3. Use the **chargeback date** to determine the chargeback month.
4. Combine approved transactions and chargebacks using `UNION ALL`.
5. Group the combined data by month and country.
6. Sum each metric separately.
7. Exclude rows where all four metrics are zero.

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(n + m)` *(processes transactions and chargebacks once)*

**Space Complexity:** `O(n + m)` *(stores the combined intermediate records)*
