## 🚀 3705. Find Golden Hour Customers

### 📖 Problem

Write a solution to find **golden hour customers** - customers who consistently order during peak hours and provide high satisfaction. A customer is a **golden hour customer** if they meet ALL the following criteria:

- Made **at least** `3` orders.
- **At least** `60%` of their orders are during **peak hours** (`11:00`-`14:00` or `18:00`-`21:00`).
- Their **average rating** for rated orders is at least `4.0`, round it to `2` decimal places.
- Have rated **at least** `50%` of their orders.

Return the result table ordered by `average_rating` in ***descending*** order, then by `customer_id`​​​​​​​ in ***descending*** order.

### 💡 Idea

The goal is to identify **golden hour customers**.

A customer qualifies if they:

- Made at least **3 orders**.
- Placed at least **60%** of their orders during peak hours (11:00–14:00 or 18:00–21:00).
- Rated at least **50%** of their orders.
- Have an average rating of at least **4.0**.

We first calculate all the required customer statistics in a CTE and then filter only those customers who satisfy all the conditions.

---

### 💻 MySQL Solution

```sql
WITH cte AS (
    SELECT customer_id,
    COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) AS total_orders,
    SUM(CASE WHEN (DATE_FORMAT(order_timestamp, '%H:%i') BETWEEN '11:00' AND '14:00')
        OR (DATE_FORMAT(order_timestamp, '%H:%i') BETWEEN '18:00' AND '21:00')
        THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS peak_hours,
    ROUND((SUM(CASE WHEN (DATE_FORMAT(order_timestamp, '%H:%i') BETWEEN '11:00' AND '14:00')
              OR (DATE_FORMAT(order_timestamp, '%H:%i') BETWEEN '18:00' AND '21:00')
              THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)) * 100, 2) AS peak_hour_percentage,
    COUNT(order_rating) AS total_ratings,
    ROUND((COUNT(order_rating) / COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)) * 100, 2) AS rating_completion,
    ROUND(AVG(order_rating), 2) AS average_rating
    FROM restaurant_orders
    GROUP BY customer_id
)
SELECT customer_id, total_orders, peak_hour_percentage, average_rating
FROM cte
WHERE total_orders >= 3
AND peak_hour_percentage >= 60
AND average_rating >= 4.0
AND rating_completion >= 50
ORDER BY average_rating DESC, customer_id DESC;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

1. The `cte` groups all orders by `customer_id`.
2. `COUNT(DISTINCT order_id)` calculates the total number of orders.
3. A `CASE` expression counts how many orders were placed during the lunch (11:00–14:00) or dinner (18:00–21:00) peak hours.
4. `peak_hour_percentage` calculates the percentage of peak-hour orders.
5. `COUNT(order_rating)` counts only non-NULL ratings.
6. `rating_completion` calculates the percentage of orders that received ratings.
7. `AVG(order_rating)` computes the average rating of all rated orders.
8. Filter customers who satisfy all the required conditions.
9. Sort the result by average rating (descending) and customer ID (descending).

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(n)`

- `n` = number of rows in the `restaurant_orders` table.

**Space Complexity:** `O(n)`

- Used to store the aggregated statistics for each customer in the CTE.
