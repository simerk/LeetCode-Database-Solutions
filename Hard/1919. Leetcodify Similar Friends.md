## 🚀 1919. Leetcodify Similar Friends 🔒

### 📖 Problem

Write a solution to report the similar friends of Leetcodify users. A user `x` and user `y` are similar friends if:

- Users `x` and `y` are friends, and
- Users `x` and `y` listened to the same three or more different songs **on the same day**.

Return the result table in **any order**. Note that you must return the similar pairs of friends the same way they were represented in the input (i.e., always `user1_id < user2_id`).

---

### 📝 Example

```text
Input: 
Listens table:                        Friendship table:
+---------+---------+------------+    +----------+----------+
| user_id | song_id | day        |    | user1_id | user2_id |
+---------+---------+------------+    +----------+----------+
| 1       | 10      | 2021-03-15 |    | 1        | 2        |
| 1       | 11      | 2021-03-15 |    | 2        | 4        |
| 1       | 12      | 2021-03-15 |    | 2        | 5        |
| 2       | 10      | 2021-03-15 |    +----------+----------+
| 2       | 11      | 2021-03-15 |
| 2       | 12      | 2021-03-15 |
| 3       | 10      | 2021-03-15 |
| 3       | 11      | 2021-03-15 |
| 3       | 12      | 2021-03-15 |
| 4       | 10      | 2021-03-15 |
| 4       | 11      | 2021-03-15 |
| 4       | 13      | 2021-03-15 |
| 5       | 10      | 2021-03-16 |
| 5       | 11      | 2021-03-16 |
| 5       | 12      | 2021-03-16 |
+---------+---------+------------+
Output: 
+----------+----------+
| user1_id | user2_id |
+----------+----------+
| 1        | 2        |
+----------+----------+
Explanation: 
Users 1 and 2 are friends, and they listened to songs 10, 11, and 12 on the same day. They are similar friends.
Users 1 and 3 listened to songs 10, 11, and 12 on the same day, but they are not friends.
Users 2 and 4 are friends, but they did not listen to the same three different songs.
Users 2 and 5 are friends and listened to songs 10, 11, and 12, but they did not listen to them on the same day.
```
---

### 💡 Idea

* Join `Friendship` with `Listens` twice to compare the listening activity of each pair of friends.
* Match when they listened to the **same song on the same day**.
* Group by the friendship pair and day, keeping pairs with at least **3 different songs**.
* Since `Friendship` already stores pairs as `user1_id < user2_id`, we preserve that order.
* Use `DISTINCT` so a pair is returned only once even if they shared 3+ songs on multiple days.

---

### 💻 Solution

```sql
SELECT DISTINCT f.user1_id, f.user2_id
FROM Friendship f
JOIN Listens l1 ON f.user1_id = l1.user_id
JOIN Listens l2
    ON f.user2_id = l2.user_id
    AND l1.song_id = l2.song_id
    AND l1.day = l2.day
GROUP BY f.user1_id, f.user2_id, l1.day
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT l1.song_id) >= 3;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

1. Start with each pair of friends from `Friendship`.
2. Match their listening records when both the **song and day** are the same.
3. Group each friendship pair by the day they listened together.
4. Keep pairs that shared at least **3 different songs on the same day**.
5. `DISTINCT` ensures each friendship pair appears only once.
6. The original `user1_id < user2_id` order from `Friendship` is preserved.

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(n²)` *(due to matching listening records between friends)*

**Space Complexity:** `O(n)` *(for intermediate grouped results)*
