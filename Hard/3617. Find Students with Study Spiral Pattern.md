## 🚀 3617. Find Students with Study Spiral Pattern

### 📖 Problem

Write a solution to find students who follow the **Study Spiral Pattern** - students who consistently study multiple subjects in a rotating cycle.

- A Study Spiral Pattern means a student studies at least `3` **different subjects** in a repeating sequence
- The pattern must repeat for **at least** `2` **complete cycles** (minimum `6` study sessions)
- Sessions must be **consecutive dates** with no gaps longer than `2` days between sessions
- Calculate the **cycle length** (number of different subjects in the pattern)
- Calculate the **total study hours** across all sessions in the pattern
- Only include students with cycle length of **at least** `3` **subjects**
  
Return the result table ordered by `cycle length` in **descending** order, then by `total study hours` in **descending** order.

### 💡 Idea

The goal is to identify students whose study sessions follow a **repeating subject pattern (Study Spiral Pattern)**.

A valid pattern must satisfy the following conditions:

- The student studies at least **3 different subjects**.
- The sequence of subjects repeats for **at least 2 complete cycles**.
- Consecutive study sessions have **no gap greater than 2 days**.
- Calculate the cycle length (number of unique subjects) and the total study hours.

The solution uses three CTEs to:
1. Order every student's study sessions.
2. Compute statistics about the study pattern.
3. Verify that the subject sequence repeats correctly.

---

## 💻 Solution

```sql
WITH ordered AS (
    SELECT student_id, subject, session_date, hours_studied,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY student_id ORDER BY session_date) AS rn,
    DATEDIFF(session_date, LAG(session_date) OVER (PARTITION BY student_id ORDER BY session_date)) AS gap
    FROM study_sessions
),
stats AS (
    SELECT student_id,
    COUNT(*) AS sessions,
    COUNT(DISTINCT subject) AS cycle_length,
    COUNT(*)/COUNT(DISTINCT subject) AS cycles,
    MAX(gap) AS max_gap,
    SUM(hours_studied) AS total_study_hours
    FROM ordered
    GROUP BY student_id
),
periodic_check AS (
    SELECT o.student_id, MIN(o.subject = prev.subject) AS is_periodic
    FROM ordered o
    JOIN stats st ON st.student_id = o.student_id
    JOIN ordered prev ON prev.student_id = o.student_id AND prev.rn = o.rn - st.cycle_length
    WHERE o.rn > st.cycle_length
    GROUP BY o.student_id
)
SELECT s.student_id, s.student_name, s.major, st.cycle_length, st.total_study_hours
FROM students s
JOIN stats st ON st.student_id = s.student_id
JOIN periodic_check pc ON pc.student_id = s.student_id
WHERE st.cycle_length >= 3 AND st.cycles >= 2 AND st.max_gap <= 2 AND pc.is_periodic = 1
ORDER BY st.cycle_length DESC, st.total_study_hours DESC;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

#### **Step 1: `ordered` CTE**

This CTE arranges each student's study sessions in chronological order.

- `ROW_NUMBER()` assigns a sequence number (`rn`) to every study session.
- `LAG()` retrieves the previous session's date.
- `DATEDIFF()` calculates the number of days between consecutive sessions (`gap`).

This information is later used to verify that there are no gaps greater than **2 days**.

---

#### **Step 2: `stats` CTE**

This CTE calculates the overall statistics for each student.

- `COUNT(*)` → total number of study sessions.
- `COUNT(DISTINCT subject)` → number of unique subjects (cycle length).
- `COUNT(*) / COUNT(DISTINCT subject)` → number of complete cycles.
- `MAX(gap)` → largest gap between consecutive sessions.
- `SUM(hours_studied)` → total study hours across all sessions.

---

#### **Step 3: `periodic_check` CTE**

This CTE verifies whether the subject sequence actually repeats.

The join

```sql
prev.rn = o.rn - st.cycle_length
```

matches every session with the session exactly **one cycle earlier**.

For example, if the cycle length is **3**:

| rn | Subject |
|----|---------|
|1|Math|
|2|Physics|
|3|Chemistry|
|4|Math|
|5|Physics|
|6|Chemistry|

Then the comparisons are:

- Session 4 ↔ Session 1
- Session 5 ↔ Session 2
- Session 6 ↔ Session 3

The expression

```sql
o.subject = prev.subject
```

returns:

- `1` if both subjects are the same.
- `0` otherwise.

Finally,

```sql
MIN(o.subject = prev.subject)
```

returns:

- `1` if **every comparison matches**, meaning the pattern repeats perfectly.
- `0` if even one comparison fails.

---

#### **Step 4: Final Query**

The final query joins the three CTEs with the `students` table and filters students who satisfy all conditions:

- Cycle length is at least **3**.
- Pattern repeats for at least **2 cycles**.
- Maximum gap between sessions is **2 days or less**.
- The repeating pattern is valid (`is_periodic = 1`).

Finally, the result is sorted by:

1. Cycle length (descending).
2. Total study hours (descending).

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(n log n)`

- Sorting the study sessions for each student dominates the running time.
- The remaining aggregations and joins are linear.

**Space Complexity:** `O(n)`

- Used to store the intermediate CTEs.
