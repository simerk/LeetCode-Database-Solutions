## 🚀 1384. Total Sales Amount by Year 🔒

### 📖 Problem

Write a solution to report the total sales amount of each item for each year, with corresponding `product_name`, `product_id`, `report_year`, and `total_amount`.

Return the result table **ordered** by `product_id` and `report_year`.

---

### 📝 Example
```text
Input: 
Product table:                     Sales table:
+------------+--------------+      +------------+--------------+-------------+---------------------+
| product_id | product_name |      | product_id | period_start | period_end  | average_daily_sales |
+------------+--------------+      +------------+--------------+-------------+---------------------+
| 1          | LC Phone     |      | 1          | 2019-01-25   | 2019-02-28  | 100                 |
| 2          | LC T-Shirt   |      | 2          | 2018-12-01   | 2020-01-01  | 10                  |
| 3          | LC Keychain  |      | 3          | 2019-12-01   | 2020-01-31  | 1                   |
+------------+--------------+      +------------+--------------+-------------+---------------------+
Output: 
+------------+--------------+-------------+--------------+
| product_id | product_name | report_year | total_amount |
+------------+--------------+-------------+--------------+
| 1          | LC Phone     |    2019     | 3500         |
| 2          | LC T-Shirt   |    2018     | 310          |
| 2          | LC T-Shirt   |    2019     | 3650         |
| 2          | LC T-Shirt   |    2020     | 10           |
| 3          | LC Keychain  |    2019     | 31           |
| 3          | LC Keychain  |    2020     | 31           |
+------------+--------------+-------------+--------------+
Explanation: 
LC Phone was sold for the period of 2019-01-25 to 2019-02-28, and there are 35 days for this period. Total amount 35*100 = 3500. 
LC T-shirt was sold for the period of 2018-12-01 to 2020-01-01, and there are 31, 365, 1 days for years 2018, 2019 and 2020 respectively.
LC Keychain was sold for the period of 2019-12-01 to 2020-01-31, and there are 31, 31 days for years 2019 and 2020 respectively.
```
---

### 💡 Idea

A sales period can span multiple years, so we need to calculate the number of days that fall within each year. Generate the years covered by each sales period using a recursive CTE, then calculate the overlapping days for each year and multiply by `average_daily_sales`.

---

### 💻 Solution

```sql
WITH RECURSIVE sales_years AS (
    SELECT product_id, period_start, period_end, average_daily_sales,
    YEAR(period_start) AS report_year
    FROM Sales
    UNION ALL
    SELECT product_id, period_start, period_end, average_daily_sales,
    report_year + 1
    FROM sales_years
    WHERE report_year < YEAR(period_end)
)
SELECT s.product_id, p.product_name, s.report_year,
DATEDIFF(
        LEAST(s.period_end, CONCAT(s.report_year, '-12-31')),
        GREATEST(s.period_start, CONCAT(s.report_year, '-01-01'))
    ) + 1 AS days_in_year,
(DATEDIFF(
        LEAST(s.period_end, CONCAT(s.report_year, '-12-31')),
        GREATEST(s.period_start, CONCAT(s.report_year, '-01-01'))
    ) + 1) * s.average_daily_sales AS total_amount
FROM sales_years s
JOIN Product p ON s.product_id = p.product_id
ORDER BY s.product_id, s.report_year;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

1. Generate one row for every year covered by each sales period.
2. Find the actual start and end date of the sales period within that year.
3. Calculate the number of days using `DATEDIFF() + 1`.
4. Multiply the number of days by `average_daily_sales`.
5. Join with `Product` to get the product name.
6. Order the result by `product_id` and `report_year`.

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(S × Y)` *(each sales period is expanded across its covered years)*

**Space Complexity:** `O(S × Y)` *(stores the expanded year-level records)*
