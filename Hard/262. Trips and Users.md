## 🚀 262. Trips and Users

### 📖 Problem

The **cancellation rate** is computed by dividing the number of canceled (by client or driver) requests with unbanned users by the total number of requests with unbanned users on that day.

Write a solution to find the **cancellation rate** of requests with unbanned users **(both client and driver must not be banned)** each day between `"2013-10-01"` and `"2013-10-03"` with **at least** one trip. Round `Cancellation Rate` to **two decimal** points.

Return the result table in **any order**.

---

### 📝 Example
```text
Input: 
Trips table:
+----+-----------+-----------+---------+---------------------+------------+
| id | client_id | driver_id | city_id | status              | request_at |
+----+-----------+-----------+---------+---------------------+------------+
| 1  | 1         | 10        | 1       | completed           | 2013-10-01 |
| 2  | 2         | 11        | 1       | cancelled_by_driver | 2013-10-01 |
| 3  | 3         | 12        | 6       | completed           | 2013-10-01 |
| 4  | 4         | 13        | 6       | cancelled_by_client | 2013-10-01 |
| 5  | 1         | 10        | 1       | completed           | 2013-10-02 |
| 6  | 2         | 11        | 6       | completed           | 2013-10-02 |
| 7  | 3         | 12        | 6       | completed           | 2013-10-02 |
| 8  | 2         | 12        | 12      | completed           | 2013-10-03 |
| 9  | 3         | 10        | 12      | completed           | 2013-10-03 |
| 10 | 4         | 13        | 12      | cancelled_by_driver | 2013-10-03 |
+----+-----------+-----------+---------+---------------------+------------+
Users table:
+----------+--------+--------+
| users_id | banned | role   |
+----------+--------+--------+
| 1        | No     | client |
| 2        | Yes    | client |
| 3        | No     | client |
| 4        | No     | client |
| 10       | No     | driver |
| 11       | No     | driver |

| 13       | No     | driver |
+----------+--------+--------+
Output: 
+------------+-------------------+
| Day        | Cancellation Rate |
+------------+-------------------+
| 2013-10-01 | 0.33              |
| 2013-10-02 | 0.00              |
| 2013-10-03 | 0.50              |
+------------+-------------------+
Explanation: 
On 2013-10-01:
  - There were 4 requests in total, 2 of which were canceled.
  - However, the request with Id=2 was made by a banned client (User_Id=2), so it is ignored in the calculation.
  - Hence there are 3 unbanned requests in total, 1 of which was canceled.
  - The Cancellation Rate is (1 / 3) = 0.33
On 2013-10-02:
  - There were 3 requests in total, 0 of which were canceled.
  - The request with Id=6 was made by a banned client, so it is ignored.
  - Hence there are 2 unbanned requests in total, 0 of which were canceled.
  - The Cancellation Rate is (0 / 2) = 0.00
On 2013-10-03:
  - There were 3 requests in total, 1 of which was canceled.
  - The request with Id=8 was made by a banned client, so it is ignored.
  - Hence there are 2 unbanned request in total, 1 of which were canceled.
  - The Cancellation Rate is (1 / 2) = 0.50
```

---

### 💡 Idea

The goal is to calculate the **daily cancellation rate** for trips between **2013-10-01** and **2013-10-03**.

Only trips where **both the client and the driver are not banned** should be considered. We count the number of cancelled trips, divide it by the total number of valid trips for each day, and round the result to **2 decimal places**.

---

### 💻 Solution

```sql
SELECT
    t.request_at AS Day,
    ROUND( SUM(t.status <> 'completed') / COUNT(*), 2 ) AS `Cancellation Rate`
FROM Trips t
JOIN Users c ON t.client_id = c.users_id
JOIN Users d ON t.driver_id = d.users_id
WHERE c.banned = 'No' AND d.banned = 'No'
AND t.request_at BETWEEN '2013-10-01' AND '2013-10-03'
GROUP BY t.request_at;
```

---

### 🛠️ How It Works

1. Join the `Trips` table with the `Users` table twice:
   - Once to retrieve the **client** information.
   - Once to retrieve the **driver** information.
2. Filter out trips where either the client or the driver is banned.
3. Restrict the data to trips between **2013-10-01** and **2013-10-03**.
4. Group the trips by `request_at` to calculate the cancellation rate for each day.
5. `SUM(t.status <> 'completed')` counts the cancelled trips because:
   - `TRUE` → `1`
   - `FALSE` → `0`
6. Divide the number of cancelled trips by the total number of valid trips using `COUNT(*)`.
7. Round the result to **2 decimal places** using `ROUND()`.

---

### ⏱️ Complexity

**Time Complexity:** `O(n)` *(single scan with joins and grouping)*

**Space Complexity:** `O(1)` *(excluding the output result set)*
